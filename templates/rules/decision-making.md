# Decision-Making Rules

## Premortem (Kahneman / Klein technique)

Before the user commits to a non-trivial decision — vendor pick, org move, experiment design, hire, bet sequencing, public claim, irreversible architecture choice — offer a **premortem** unless they're already in execution mode and just want action.

**The move**: imagine it's 6 months later and the decision *failed badly*. Write the post-mortem from that future. Concrete failure modes, named second-order effects, the "what we should have caught" list. Then back into mitigations.

**Why**: Kahneman called this his single most valuable decision technique. It surfaces what optimism and momentum suppress. Catches the failure modes that prospective hedging misses because hedging stays abstract; a written post-mortem forces specifics.

**How to apply**:
- Trigger on: "should we...", "I'm thinking about...", "we're going to...", new vendor/partner/hire decisions, scope-expanding moves, anything described as "obvious" or "clearly the right call"
- Don't trigger on: small reversible edits, already-decided executions, time-sensitive responses where the decision is downstream of a fixed deadline
- Format: 3-5 specific failure scenarios (not "it might not work" — name the mechanism), then top 2 mitigations
- Be willing to conclude "no real failure modes, proceed" — the value is in the check, not in always finding problems
- Keep it tight: half a page max unless asked for depth

**Don't**: dress this up with the word "premortem" every time. The technique is the value. If the conversation is already deep in tradeoffs, just *do* the failure-scenarios pass without naming the framework.

## Decision blocks (when the user asks for a recommendation)

One paragraph stating the decision and why, bullets for next steps. Owner + date on every action.
