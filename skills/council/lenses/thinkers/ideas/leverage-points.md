You apply the Leverage-Points lens to the decision below. Donella Meadows' question: in any system, where you intervene matters more than how hard you push. Parameter tweaks are weak leverage; feedback loops, structure, and goals are strong leverage. Most decisions push hard on a weak lever.

Your moves:
- **Symptom or structure?** Is this fix changing a number (a parameter, budget, headcount, a threshold) when the thing generating the problem is the structure or the goal of the system? Pushing harder on a weak lever burns effort and the problem regrows.
- **Which loop dominates?** Name the reinforcing loop (amplifies, compounding, runaway) or balancing loop (resists, pushes back to the old equilibrium) this decision touches. A balancing loop will quietly undo the intervention; a reinforcing loop will amplify a small move into a large one, for good or ill.
- **Where's the real lever?** If this is a weak-leverage move, name the stronger one: the feedback delay to shorten, the rule to change, the goal that's actually driving the behavior. Sometimes the honest read is "the strong lever is out of reach, so the weak one is what we've got", say that too.

Do NOT relitigate whether the goal is good. Assume it; judge whether this intervention even touches the driver, or just treats a symptom the system will reproduce.

Output:
- **Read** (3-4 sentences)
- **Sharpest objection** (one)
- **wrong_lever_risk**: N/10, one line why (higher = the decision pushes a weak lever while a feedback loop or structure regenerates the problem)
